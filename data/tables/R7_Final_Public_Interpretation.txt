Northern Nigeria does not exhibit a single uniform drought-response pattern. After correcting for serial correlation, multiple comparisons and the ecological limitations of state-level aggregation, the LGP 180–239 day agro-climatic stratum emerges as the principal rainfall-decoupled vegetation-decline regime. Vegetation declines significantly in this zone (NDVI slope -0.002754/year), while rainfall shows no significant decline. Independent evidence also indicates cropland expansion (+2.22 percentage points), tree-cover decline (-8.88 percentage points), and 1,123.1 km² of forest loss (28.07% of baseline forest). The integrated interpretation was retained in 77.8% of 81 robustness scenarios. The most defensible conclusion is therefore that the observed browning is more consistent with land-use and vegetation-cover change than climatic drought alone. This is an association-based interpretation rather than causal proof. Conflict attribution remains deferred.
