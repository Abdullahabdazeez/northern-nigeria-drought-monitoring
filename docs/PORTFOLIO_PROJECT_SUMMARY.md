# Vegetation Browning Beyond Climatic Drought: Agro-Climatic Trend, Rainfall Decoupling and Land-Change Evidence in Northern Nigeria

Vegetation browning is often treated as a direct sign of drought, but that interpretation can be misleading when rainfall is stable and land cover is changing. This project examined vegetation and rainfall trends across Northern Nigeria from 2001–2025 using agro-climatic length-of-growing-period zones rather than state boundaries. After correcting trend significance for serial correlation and multiple comparisons, the analysis identified the **LGP 180–239 day zone** as the clearest rainfall-decoupled vegetation-decline regime. NDVI declined significantly at **-0.002754 per year**, while rainfall showed no significant trend. EVI independently confirmed the vegetation decline. Land-change analysis then showed **+2.22 percentage-point cropland change**, **-8.88 percentage-point tree-cover change**, and **1,123.1 km² of forest loss**. The integrated interpretation remained supported in **77.8% of 81 robustness scenarios**.

## Main Result

The evidence indicates that vegetation decline in the LGP 180–239 day zone is **more consistent with land-use and vegetation-cover change than climatic drought alone**. The analysis demonstrates association rather than proof of causation.

## Why It Matters

The project shows that satellite vegetation decline should not automatically be labelled drought. Combining vegetation, rainfall and land-change evidence can help planners distinguish climatic stress from land-management pressures and target environmental interventions more appropriately.

## Tools and Methods

Google Earth Engine, Python, geospatial analysis, NDVI, EVI, rainfall time-series analysis, Sen slope, Hamed–Rao modified Mann–Kendall, TFPW fallback, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction, GAEZ agro-climatic stratification, Dynamic World, Hansen forest loss, robustness analysis.
