# Drought and Vegetation Stress Monitoring Using Remote Sensing and Time-Series Analysis — Northern Nigeria

## Study Overview

This project assessed drought exposure, vegetation condition and long-term climate–vegetation dynamics across 19 states in Northern Nigeria covering the North Central, North East and North West geopolitical zones.

The principal analysis period was 2001–2025, while CHIRPS rainfall data from 1981–2025 were used to establish a longer climatological baseline for rainfall anomalies and Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) calculations.

The analysis integrated MODIS MOD13Q1 NDVI and EVI, CHIRPS precipitation, ERA5-Land temperature, state administrative boundaries and statistical time-series methods including standardized anomalies, Vegetation Condition Index (VCI), SPI-3, SPI-6, Mann–Kendall trend tests and Sen's slope estimation.

---

## Executive Results

Northern Nigeria experienced a statistically significant regional decline in growing-season vegetation condition during 2001–2025.

Regional NDVI declined at a Sen's slope of -0.000777 NDVI units per year (p = 0.0085), while EVI declined by -0.000358 units per year (p = 0.0413).

In contrast, May–October rainfall showed a positive but statistically non-significant trend of +2.50 mm per year (p = 0.2012). Growing-season temperature increased significantly at +0.0154 °C per year (p = 0.0288), equivalent to an estimated increase of approximately 0.37 °C across the analysis period.

These contrasting trends indicate that the observed regional vegetation decline cannot be explained by changes in seasonal rainfall totals alone.

---

## Vegetation Trend Pattern

Ten of the 19 states recorded statistically significant NDVI browning.

The strongest browning occurred in Niger State, with an NDVI Sen's slope of -0.003468 per year.

Four states recorded statistically significant greening, with Borno showing the strongest positive trend at +0.002495 NDVI units per year.

North Central Nigeria displayed the clearest concentration of long-term vegetation decline, while parts of the North West and Borno exhibited significant greening tendencies.

---

## Temperature and Rainfall Trends

Nine states experienced statistically significant growing-season warming.

The strongest warming occurred in Kogi State at +0.0325 °C per year, corresponding to an estimated increase of approximately 0.78 °C during 2001–2025.

All six North Central states recorded statistically significant warming, making North Central the most consistent warming zone in the study.

No state recorded a statistically significant decline in May–October rainfall.

Four states — Jigawa, Bauchi, Borno and Yobe — recorded statistically significant rainfall increases. Yobe had the strongest increase at +5.88 mm per year.

---

## Drought and Vegetation Stress

SPI-3 and SPI-6 analysis showed substantial interannual and spatial variability in drought exposure.

The most spatially extensive compound drought–vegetation stress event occurred in 2015.

During 2015:

- all 19 states experienced at least one May–October SPI-6 drought month;
- 12 states experienced significant vegetation stress;
- all 12 vegetation-stressed states simultaneously experienced SPI-6 drought;
- all 12 compound events included severe SPI-6 drought conditions.

At the regional scale, 2015 also recorded the lowest growing-season NDVI in the 2001–2025 series, with an NDVI z-score of -2.50 and VCI of 0.

---

## Compound Drought–Vegetation Hotspots

The five Very High compound drought–vegetation hotspot states were:

1. Kaduna — 80.23
2. Niger — 77.24
3. Benue — 70.04
4. Kebbi — 64.62
5. Nasarawa — 64.55

These states combined relatively frequent SPI-6 drought exposure with repeated vegetation-stress responses.

---

## Integrated Climate–Vegetation Pressure

The final integrated pressure analysis combined:

- NDVI browning intensity;
- growing-season temperature trend;
- SPI-6 drought frequency;
- compound drought–vegetation stress.

The five Very High integrated-pressure states were:

1. Nasarawa — 85.01
2. Niger — 80.31
3. Adamawa — 75.68
4. Kaduna — 70.83
5. Kwara — 70.76

North Central recorded the highest mean integrated pressure score of 69.17, compared with 48.42 in the North East and 44.65 in the North West.

---

## Climate–Vegetation Decoupling

Seven states recorded statistically significant vegetation browning despite an increasing May–October rainfall trend:

Niger, Gombe, Nasarawa, Kebbi, Kwara, Bauchi, Plateau.

This result is important because it indicates that rainfall totals alone do not adequately explain the vegetation changes observed in these states.

Potential contributing mechanisms that require further targeted investigation include:

- increasing thermal stress and evapotranspiration demand;
- rainfall timing and intra-seasonal distribution;
- land-use and land-cover change;
- agricultural intensification;
- grazing pressure;
- deforestation and woodland degradation;
- soil degradation;
- vegetation recovery dynamics following drought.

The analysis demonstrates statistical association and spatial coincidence, not direct causation.

---

## Planning Implications

### 1. Prioritise North Central for climate–vegetation resilience interventions

North Central had the highest integrated pressure and the strongest concentration of simultaneous vegetation browning and warming.

Nasarawa and Niger should receive particularly high planning attention because they combine significant vegetation decline, warming, repeated drought exposure and Very High integrated pressure.

### 2. Move beyond seasonal rainfall totals in drought monitoring

The 2015 event demonstrated that severe intra-seasonal drought can occur even when total May–October rainfall is not exceptionally low.

Operational drought monitoring should therefore combine rainfall totals with SPI-3, SPI-6, vegetation anomalies and temperature indicators.

### 3. Establish multi-indicator early-warning systems

A practical monitoring framework should combine:

- CHIRPS rainfall anomalies;
- SPI-3 and SPI-6;
- MODIS NDVI/EVI anomalies;
- VCI;
- ERA5-Land temperature anomalies;
- recurrent hotspot information.

This would allow planners to distinguish meteorological drought from vegetation stress and identify areas where the two occur together.

### 4. Target recurrent hotspot states

Kaduna, Niger, Benue, Kebbi and Nasarawa showed the highest compound drought–vegetation hotspot scores.

These states are appropriate candidates for more detailed local-scale assessment of agricultural drought, rangeland condition, water availability and land degradation.

### 5. Investigate browning despite increasing rainfall

Niger, Nasarawa, Kebbi and Kwara are particularly important because they combine high or very-high pressure with vegetation browning despite non-decreasing rainfall.

Planning responses in such areas should include land-cover change assessment, soil-condition monitoring, agricultural pressure analysis and investigation of temperature-driven moisture stress.

### 6. Recognise contrasting greening trajectories

Significant greening in Borno, Katsina, Zamfara and Sokoto demonstrates that Northern Nigeria does not exhibit a single uniform vegetation trajectory.

Planning and environmental management should therefore avoid treating the entire region as ecologically homogeneous.

---

## Methodological Cautions

The analysis uses state-level spatial aggregation for several statistical indicators. Local conditions within individual states may differ substantially from state averages.

VCI values are relative to each area's observed 2001–2025 vegetation range and should not be interpreted as an absolute measure of ecosystem health.

The integrated pressure and compound hotspot scores are project-specific planning diagnostics derived from normalized indicators. They are not internationally standardized drought indices.

Observed relationships between rainfall, temperature and vegetation do not by themselves demonstrate causality.

Further work should incorporate land-use/land-cover change, soil moisture, evapotranspiration, agricultural production and finer administrative units where reliable data are available.

---

## Conclusion

The project demonstrates how satellite remote sensing, climate datasets, drought indices and time-series statistics can be integrated to identify climate-sensitive vegetation dynamics across Northern Nigeria.

The analysis detected a significant regional decline in NDVI and EVI, significant regional warming, recurrent drought exposure and strong spatial variation in vegetation response.

North Central emerged as the highest overall climate–vegetation pressure zone, while Nasarawa, Niger, Adamawa, Kaduna and Kwara represented the highest integrated state-level priorities.

The results also reveal that vegetation browning frequently occurs without declining seasonal rainfall, highlighting the importance of temperature, drought timing and non-climatic land pressures in environmental monitoring and spatial planning.

These outputs provide a reproducible geospatial framework for drought monitoring, land-degradation assessment, climate adaptation planning and environmental decision support.
